# A8 – Gears

## Objective

The objective for this assignment is to mathematically solve then design a gearbox that can be both assembled and disassembled given the specific gears. The gears have a ratio of 24:9 with torque speeds 0.125 and 0.25 inches with both having a 1 inch length. Using McMaster and the engineering handbook for the specifications and fits then finally CAD designing the final product. The 2157 portion will then require 6 different drawings to outline each part of the gearbox.

The first major section of the assignment was to mathematically solve for the unknowns of the given problem. These being the input and output torques along with the speeds. All of this was to make sure that the diameters for the rods would be able to hold the force the gears would be generating. This was a fairly straight forward process and showed than a 0.125 inch and 0.250 inch shaft would be perfect for the job. We then moved onto solving for the bearings that would have to hold the shafts inside the gearbox casing. Given the diameter and speed the equations showed that the forces would less than the max strength of the material chosen which would be a oil enriched bronze.

- CAD Design:

The CAD design was both the most enjoyable and most challenging part of the project. After parametrically modeling the body and lid I used construction geometries to lay out where I wanted the holes for the shafts as well as the screws. I then used hole wizard to add a #6-32 screw as it would fit and not be so big to make the material deform. But the hole geometries showed that I needed to make the height of the casing larger for everything to fit. For the material I decided on ASTM A36 steel as it is common and is the first thing that came to mind. Given that the casing material was not a big issue I went safe though some plastics and lighter metals might make the design more optimized. I then made sperate parts for the 0.125x1in and 0.250x1in shafts. Gathering all of the parts including those from McMaster I turned to the assembly to put the pieces together. After mating all of the parts in where they were supposed to go I realized that I made the width of the case to small to fit the gears so I had to change that. After getting all of the parts into place I used the Solidworks Toolbox to add 4 #6-32 finishing off the design.
